# Agile-Scrum
Agile/Scrum software development project demonstrating iterative development, user stories, sprint planning, team collaboration, and SDLC best practices.

1) How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
   
I interpret user needs by first trying to understand the problem from the user's perspective rather than immediately thinking about how I am        going to code a solution. Once I understand what the user is trying to accomplish, I can break those needs down into specific features and          requirements. User stories help with this because they describe a feature from the user's point of view and explain what the user needs and why. They also provide a manageable way to divide a larger project into smaller pieces of functionality that can be developed, tested, and improved throughout the development process.
   
2) How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by breaking a larger problem into smaller, manageable pieces instead of trying to design and build everything at once. One of the most useful things I learned from Agile and Scrum is the value of iterative development. Building software in smaller increments makes it easier to test functionality, identify problems, and adjust the design as requirements change. In my future development work, I want to keep using practices such as maintaining a prioritized backlog, developing software in short iterations, gathering feedback regularly, and reflecting on what worked and what could be improved. I think these practices can make the development process more organized while still allowing the flexibility needed when a project changes.

3) What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means being dependable, communicating clearly, and recognizing that developing software is usually a collaborative effort. Each team member may have different responsibilities, but everyone is working toward the same product goal. A good team member should communicate problems instead of letting them become larger issues, be willing to listen to feedback, and help other team members when possible. I also think accountability is important. If I commit to completing a task during a Sprint, the rest of the team should be able to depend on me to complete it, or I should communicate early if something is preventing me from doing so. Agile development showed me that a successful team depends as much on communication and collaboration as on individual technical ability.
